Photosynthesis is the process plants use to convert light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars.

It takes place mainly in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light, especially red and blue wavelengths, and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.

The overall equation is:
carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose + oxygen.

There are two main stages.

Light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. Light splits water (photolysis), releasing oxygen. ATP and NADPH are produced.

The Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions) occurs in the stroma. Rubisco fixes carbon dioxide onto ribulose bisphosphate. ATP and NADPH from the light reactions reduce the carbon compounds to form glucose.

Factors that affect the rate include light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. At high light intensity the process may be limited by carbon dioxide or by temperature.

Stomata allow carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapour out. On hot dry days stomata may close, slowing photosynthesis.
